v2.1.0 · updated 3 November 2026 · launch client 1.60.1.70707 + Blizzard’s 2 November development notes · for the 4 November 2026 launch

1. Overview, Ruleset & Horde Races

Ruleset determined: World of Warcraft: Forever is Blizzard’s own standalone “Classic+” branch (runs alongside retail and Classic). It is set after Warcraft III Reforged: Forsaken Kingdom and before Molten Core, uses reworked 1.12-style talent trees, and has a permanent level cap of 60. It runs on the modern WoW client (beta patch 1.60.1, interface 16001). It is NOT a straight Classic Era / 1.12 re-release.
v2.0.0 (31 Oct 2026) rebuilds this guide on the data verified for the Horde BiC Picks guide, including the 30 Oct datamined server hotfixes. What changed in v2.1.0 (3 Nov 2026)

Blizzard’s pre-launch class notes are in. On 3 Nov Blizzard posted its 2 November development notes Blizzard. Every Hunter number they touch now uses Blizzard’s value; the earlier datamined Aspect of the Hawk and Trueshot Aura cuts are gone from this guide.

  • Trueshot Aura: ranks 1–5 give +20 / 35 / 50 / 75 / 100 ranged attack power Blizzard.
  • Shots: Aimed Shot bonus damage +~110%; Arcane Shot base damage +~30% and attack-power coefficient 0.10 → 0.15; Serpent Sting 5% of attack power per tick (was 3%) and ~20% more base damage Blizzard.
  • Talents: Barrage 5 / 10 / 15% (was 3 / 7 / 10); Sniper Shot bonus damage +100%; Lightning Reflexes 3 / 6 / 9 / 12 / 15% Agility (was 2–10%); Lacerating Strikes always overwrites your own existing effect; Pathfinding in row 3 is official Blizzard.

  • Hunter’s Mark: different ranks can no longer stack on one target, and rank 4 now gives more ranged attack power than rank 3 Blizzard.
  • Proc roots (Entrapment, Counterattack, Improved Wing Clip, Improved Hamstring, Frostbite) now have diminishing returns Blizzard.
  • Pets keep their innate attack-speed passive when you untrain them Blizzard. Orc Command (+5% pet damage) is restated in the notes Blizzard.
  • Tuning: Blizzard raised class damage and the health of higher-level creatures together, and plans no big tuning before the raids next month Blizzard. 2. Leveling (1–60)

Spec choice

Recommended: Beast Mastery. Community guides that tested the 1–30 beta rate it the fastest and safest leveling spec: the pet tanks, two Summon Hawks plus Auto Shot do the damage. Marksmanship is rated below BM for solo leveling; Survival is a melee spec that “only comes together past 30”. With dual spec (Overview: at launch vs level 40), the plan for this guide’s Orc is BM for questing and Survival on the second spec for world PvP at 60; Marksmanship is the raid spec theory. WoW Forever DB rates Beast Mastery S for leveling (30 Oct) community.

Pet choice & taming

  • Taming quest at 10. Orc & Troll: Thotar in Razor Hill (Dire Mottled Boar, Surf Crawler, Armored Scorpid), finish with Ormak Grimshot in Orgrimmar. Tauren: Yaw Sharpmane at Bloodhoof (Adult Plainstrider, Prairie Stalker, Swoop), finish with Holt Thunderhorn in Thunder Bluff. Windshaper Skyborne: Quel’ana Quickgale in Valanaar on Zephras Isle (Vuldren Alpha — a Fox, Windsong Crawler, Ornery Galestrider).
  • Since 24 Sep you cannot tame a Beast above your own level. Tame at 10, then upgrade as you find better pets.
  • Tank pet: Bear (Swipe hits 3 targets) or Turtle (Shell Shield). Oasis Snapjaws in the Barrens are a level 15–16 Turtle (community report).
  • Damage pet: fast Cat or Wolf; check attack speed with Beast Lore (24). Stranglethorn Vale has better Cats, Raptors and the first Gorillas.
  • Utility: Boar (Charge + immobilize), Wolf (Furious Howl, party melee AP; reduced 40% in beta), Spider (Web root), Crocolisk (Dismember, −25% healing received for 6 s, 15 s cooldown; niche).
  • Pets scale with your gear (community data: 2 HP per point of your Stamina, 30% of your armor, 10% of your AP, and your higher melee/ranged crit) — exact ratios unverified / needs confirmation after launch.
  • Keep the pet fed and happy: an unhappy pet deals 25% less damage (Classic rule, not restated by Blizzard: unverified / needs confirmation after launch). Diets differ by family (Bear/Boar eat almost anything; Cat meat/fish; Wolf meat).
  • Full pet ranking, tame spots by level and threat tips: section 8, Pet Evaluator.

Leveling rotation

  • Hunter’s Mark → send pet → Serpent Sting → Auto Shot, with Aimed Shot (from 20) and Summon Hawk (from 25) on cooldown.
  • Keep two hawks up. Summon Hawk shares its 6 s cooldown with Arcane Shot, so Arcane Shot is only for spare mana as BM.
  • Stop moving a beat before you shoot: Auto Shot needs 0.5 s standing still. Your abilities no longer clip Auto Shot.
  • Inside 8 yards: Raptor Strike, Wing Clip, step back. Disengage (20) now drops twice the threat but does not drop combat.
  • From 30, Feign Death resets bad pulls. Do not stay down 6 minutes — the tooltip says you really die.
  • Out of combat use Aspect of the Cheetah (dazed when hit); in combat Aspect of the Hawk.
  • Aspect of the Hawk (Blizzard, 2 Nov notes): ranks 1–6 give +20 / 35 / 50 / 70 / 90 / 110 ranged attack power Blizzard, learned at levels 10 / 18 / 28 / 38 / 48 / 58 community. Blizzard’s list stops at rank 6; the client also lists a level-60 rank 7 whose value Blizzard has not restated clientunverified. It is your leveling aspect; with the pet doing much of the work, BM leveling still leans on the pet theory.
  • Pack or elite pull: swap the Cat for a Carrion Bird (section 8). Demoralizing Screech lowers the melee attack power of every enemy in melee range for 30 s client.

Gear, weapons & ammo

  • Carry a lot of ammo (arrows for bows/crossbows, bullets for guns). One beta tester used 1,325 arrows in a single Deadmines run.
  • Level 20 quiver: quest Vyrin’s Revenge rewards a Hunting Quiver or Hunting Ammo Sack (+10% ranged attack speed). It still takes a normal bag slot.
  • Horde quest guns/bows reported in beta: Misplaced Shooter (13), Harvester’s Pestslayer (15), Daryl’s Hunting Bow/Rifle (16), Walton’s Second-Favorite Gun (18); Privateer Musket / Owlsight Rifle (20), Bow of Plunder (28), Cliffrunner’s Aim and Raptor’s End (29), Lunaris Bow (30).
  • Truthseeker’s Bow (Library-book quest “Greater Friend of the Library”): Blizzard’s 8 Oct notes set its level requirement to 40. Some community pages still say “no level requirement” — trust the official note. Horde turn-in: Owen Thadd, Magic Quarter, Undercity.
  • Dungeon option at ~30: Golemsight Long Gun (Excavation Site: Wetlands) — community report.
  • Mail armor from 40; leather before that. Stat priority while leveling: ranged weapon DPS, then Agility.

Quest & leveling tips

  • Cozy Sleeping Bag: scavenger hunt from level 14 in the Barrens (Horde) — +3% XP buff and a portable rest spot.
  • Camping unlocks around level 5 (quest “The Great Outdoors” outside the starting hub); other players’ tradeskills give buffs at your campfire.
  • Library books are open to all classes (Horde turn-in: Owen Thadd, Undercity) — rewards include Truthseeker’s Bow.
  • Dungeon quest bonus XP was cut 50% on 1 Oct, but dungeon mob XP was raised ~20% on 8 Oct and the party-level XP penalty was reverted to Classic behaviour.
  • Since 15 Oct the Horde is attacked on sight in outdoor Dalaran; the City of Dalaran dungeon is still accessible.
  • Legacy Adventure tree: Well Rested (more rested XP, higher cap). Hunter class quests at 50 (Ogtinc, Azshara) and the Rhok’delar chain at 60 exist in client data only: unverified / needs confirmation after launch.

Professions

  • Tauren: Cultivation duplicates herb nodes (now level-gated) — good synergy with Herbalism/Alchemy for gold.

Optimize your leveling with Factoruide

Factoruide is a free third-party leveling planner. It doesn’t give you one route written for everyone: it simulates the whole climb for the character you describe (travel, fights, loot, quest and mob XP, trainers, professions, gear) and searches for the fastest route to your target level. It supports WoW Forever, Classic Era/Hardcore and TBC Anniversary, and the guide is computed in your browser. The Factoruide Guide addon (CurseForge, opened with /fg or AddOns → Factoruide) then follows the route in game. It can accept and turn in quests, handle trainers, flights, selling and repairs, point a compass to the next step, and compare your time with the guide’s.

Hunter setup, step by step:

  • Character: choose your Horde race (this guide’s leveling pick is Orc) and Hunter. To start from an existing character, install Factoruide Profile, type /fg, choose “Export my character” and paste the text into New guide.
  • Professions: Skinning + Leatherworking, the pairing this guide recommends for leveling. Set the skill to reach at each level.
  • Zones: prefer this guide’s route: Durotar/Mulgore → Barrens → Hillsbrad → Ashenvale → Thousand Needles.
  • Dungeons: leave them on automatic (click once to force, twice to exclude). Force Excavation Site: Wetlands if you want the Golemsight Long Gun.
  • Play style: risk Normal (yellow quests, never orange) suits a pet-tanking BM Hunter. Allow Grind on the way, because the pet makes mob kills cheap (analysis).
  • No Hunter settings: the app has no pet, ammo or kiting options. Budget ammo yourself; the planner’s bag-space setting may help keep room for ammo (analysis).
  • Export: on the Guides page choose Export → Addon, then in game type /fg and choose “Import a guide…”.
Factoruide doesn’t plan talents or specs. It has no talent or spec settings, so follow this guide’s BM order (31/20/0) and pet advice. Factoruide only plans the route. BM leveling evaluation (Theorycraft)

Goal: maximum kill speed without the Hunter pulling aggro. On a single target the two goals point the same way: the pet’s threat comes from its damage (plus Growl), so a pet that deals more damage both kills faster and builds a bigger threat lead. The Hunter pulls aggro when their threat exceeds the pet’s by the pull-off margin; in Classic Era that margin is 130% at range and 110% in melee (Classic (assumed) unverified / needs confirmation after launch).

2 Nov change: Blizzard raised Hunter shot damage (Aimed Shot bonus +~110%, Arcane Shot +~30% with a bigger attack-power share, Serpent Sting 5% attack power per tick) and announced no matching pet change [8] Blizzard. Your threat now grows faster relative to the pet’s, so the pull-off margin is tighter: let the pet build 2–3 s of lead before Aimed Shot, and favour the higher-damage pets, because more pet damage is also more pet threat (Theorycraft). Threat tips for leveling

  • Keep Growl on autocast when solo and turn it off in groups [89, 51]. Growl costs 15 Focus on a 5 s cooldown; its threat value is set by the server and isn’t in client data [69] unverified / needs confirmation after launch
  • Send the pet first and let Growl plus one Claw/Bite land before your first shot (about 2–3 s). Auto Shot needs 0.5 s standing still anyway (Theorycraft).
  • Don’t open with Aimed Shot or Blood Fury. Start with Auto Shot and Serpent Sting, then burst once the pet has a lead. A beta tester reports it is “kinda easy to pull aggro off your pet if you get a string of crits” [91].
  • Autocast Claw (25 Focus, no cooldown) and Bite (35 Focus, 10 s cooldown) [70, 71]; don’t run a third Focus spender on autocast or the pet starves for Focus [89].
  • Intimidation “generates high threat” (1 min cooldown) and stuns 3 s: use it to pull a loose mob back onto the pet [72].
  • Bestial Wrath (+50% pet damage for 18 s) also raises the pet’s threat from damage by 50% while it lasts (Theorycraft) [73].
  • Keep the pet happy: 125% damage happy vs 75% unhappy also means 125% vs 75% of the threat from damage [51] (Theorycraft for the threat part).
  • If you do pull aggro: Disengage (threat reduction doubled [3]; it turns off auto-attack, so restart Auto Shot) or Feign Death.
  • Summon Hawk: hawks deal damage only and are not tanks clienttheory; whether their damage adds threat to you is not documented unverified / needs confirmation after launch. Watch the threat meter when you start using them at 25.
  • Use Defensive mode when soloing near other packs; Aggressive mode came back on 1 Oct and new tames reportedly start on Aggressive [8, 51].
